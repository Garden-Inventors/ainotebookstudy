What did the guided group actually do?

According to the study, the guided group did these five things

In a weekly 15-minute session with the teacher:

  • The instructor showed how to use NotebookLM well
  • Coaching on writing and refining prompts
  • Strategies for checking AI answers
  • Group discussion of AI responses, with misunderstandings cleared up
  • Practice on their own, including finding their own errors

Together, these may have contributed to the guided group’s extra +4.0 point gain (+13.1, compared with +9.1 for independent use).

Here’s the catch: the study bundled all five together, so we don’t know which one had value.

Putting it together

  • On its own, NotebookLM beat teaching alone on achievement (+9.1 vs. +5.8).
  • Weekly teacher guidance added more for achievement (+13.1), but not for test anxiety or confidence.
  • It’s one study: six weeks, one university, and a bundle of changes. Promising, not settled.

Setup. 118 education undergraduates took the same research methods course for six weeks. Groups: teaching alone, teaching plus NotebookLM on their own, and teaching plus NotebookLM with a weekly 15-minute teacher session. Measured: achievement, test anxiety, and self-efficacy.

Results. Achievement gain: +5.8, +9.1, +13.1. Test anxiety change: −2.0, −9.3, −13.4. Self-efficacy change: +2.1, +9.9, +9.4. Guidance added to achievement only.

Guided session. The instructor modeled effective use, coached prompt writing, taught ways to check AI answers, led group discussion of AI responses, and had students practice finding their own errors.

Caveats. Short term only; confidence may be inflated; the guided group got extra instructor time; the guidance was a bundle; one university, six weeks, a researcher-made test, and very large effect sizes.
